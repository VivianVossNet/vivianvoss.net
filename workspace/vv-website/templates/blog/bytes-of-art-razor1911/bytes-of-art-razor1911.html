Razor1911 by Razor 1911

Forty years of scene history, told by the people who were there, in the techniques of each decade it passes through. Bytes of Art.

demoscene demo amiga cracktro

Twenty-six productions ran in the PC demo competition at Revision this Easter, and one of them was named after the group that made it. It won with 1,621 points. Second place finished on 1,259. The room also gave it the crowd favourite award, which is the vote nobody can lobby for.

The demo is called Razor1911. So is the group. That collision of names is the whole idea.

The Work

Ten minutes, on Windows, released at the party on 5 April 2026. Dubmood and Goto80 led it and worked on it for months, with Flopine and Anat as the other credited creators and roughly twenty more names behind the code, the graphics and the music. The stated aim was to bring the group's history to life.

That credit list looks bloated until you read what is in it. PETSCII, drawn by Dino. ANSI and ASCII, drawn by Asphyx. Those are the letterform technologies of the Commodore 64 and of the bulletin boards, decades apart, and both were wanted here by name. Goto80 puts his own range at purist PETSCII through to what he cheerfully calls blasphemous post-textmode, which is the sound of someone handling an old technique, not imitating one. A retrospective built this way needs one specialist per era, and the scene is one of the few places left where all of those people are alive and reachable.

The Roots, and a Number in Hexadecimal

October 1985, Norway. Three teenagers, later known as Doctor No, Insane TTM and Sector9, start a group on the Commodore 64 and call it Razor 2992. The name does not survive the year. They change it to 1911, because 1911 in hexadecimal reads 777, and at that age this is an entirely sufficient reason.

In 1987 the group re-forms on the Amiga, at the Danish Gold Copy Party. The early nineties bring the move to the PC. Demozoo lists over three hundred productions under the name, spanning roughly 1988 to 2012, across cracktros, intros, demos and musicdisks.

The Cracktro as a Form

The cracktro deserves its own paragraph, because it is where the constraint lived.

A cracktro is the small screen that plays before a cracked game loads. It carries the group's name, a scroller, usually a tune, and it has to do all of that without keeping the person waiting and without eating memory the game will want back. One screen, a few kilobytes, a few seconds of goodwill. That is a tighter brief than most demos ever accept, and it produced a house style that outlived its purpose entirely: the scroller riding its sine wave, and underneath it the tune that loops until somebody presses a key.

The demoscene grew out of that practice. The signature came first and the artwork grew around it until the artwork was the point, and groups began releasing productions with nothing attached to them at all. Razor 1911 sits at that hinge, which is why a retrospective about this group cannot be a demo history with the cracking edited out. The United States Department of Justice has described them as the oldest software cracking group still active on the internet, and Operation Buccaneer in 2001 reached over sixty suspects internationally. A former leader was sentenced in 2003, and in 2004 Sean Michael Breen received fifty months. Those years are part of why the demo division re-formed as its own line in 1993, with Colorbird as the only original member left in it.

The Amiga Years

The work from that period still holds up. Voyage, released on 28 December 1991 for Amiga OCS and ECS, took third place in the Amiga demo competition at The Party 1991: code by Sim, graphics by Drake and Murdock, music by Codex, Tip and Mantronix of Phenomena guesting. Chaosmaze took second at The Gathering 1994. On the PC, Falu Red Color took second at Assembly the same year.

And the demo division kept going long after the headlines stopped. In 2011 it won the public choice award at the Scene.org Awards for the 64K intro Insert No Coins, coded by Rez with music by Dubmood. Hold on to those two handles.

The Event

Revision runs each Easter in Saarbrücken, Germany, and is the largest pure demoscene event in the world, drawing around eight hundred people. The 2026 edition ran from 3 to 6 April under the motto The Power Within.

The Point

Rez coded the 2011 intro and appears again in the 2026 credits. Dubmood wrote the music for that intro and is one of the four people who led this demo. So the retrospective comes, in large part, from the people it is about, rather than from archivists working off footage.

The strongest evidence of that sits in one line of the credit list. Sector 9, from Stjørdal in Norway, is credited on this demo for graphics. Demozoo records his earliest production as a Commodore 64 intro from 1986 and his most recent as this Windows demo from April 2026, with two hundred and seventy-nine productions in between. Wikipedia names him as one of the three who started the group in 1985; the portals track productions and not founding dates, and the productions alone span forty years.

The other days of this week argue for taking things away, and they argue it with a ledger. Friday makes a quieter case. A scene whose own running joke is that it died years ago kept its people long enough that a man who drew letterforms for a cracked C64 game could draw them again, forty years later, for a production that won the largest room the scene has.

Not every kind of longevity can be measured in uptime.

  • Led by: Dubmood and Goto80 over several months, with Flopine and Anat as the other credited creators and roughly twenty further names.
