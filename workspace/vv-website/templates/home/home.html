Vivian Voss

System Architect & Software Developer. 37 years in the game. 26 professionally.

Building minimal tools that earn every byte. Inspired by the demoscene. Guided by Unix philosophy. Using AI to engineer, educate and validate, not to replicate the bloat faster.

What I Do

I design systems where reduction is the method, not the compromise. Small binaries. Semantic markup. Honest interfaces. Every abstraction must justify its weight.

Currently Senior Developer at a German DIY wholesale giant, working with React, TypeScript, and Spryker. Privately building small tools for testing ideas, ultralight and rooted in demoscene constraints.

Focus

System architecture, performance engineering, and the frontend that sits on top of both. Full-stack, if you must; I prefer to call it taking responsibility for the whole path.

The common thread is root cause over symptom: I would rather remove the reason a system is slow than buy hardware to outrun it.

I keep a German-language record of how that diagnosis is done, at advisor.vivianvoss.net: what gets measured, what a written finding contains, and where the system boundary is drawn. It documents a way of working rather than an offer; I take no mandates.

Three Series, One Argument

The writing here runs in three parallel series: Lean Software, on building less and getting more; Unix Universe, on the operating systems that outlived their critics; and IT Philosophy, on why we keep making the same expensive mistakes. Different angles, same argument: complexity is a cost, not an achievement.

Grafana, or Two Commands

A FreeBSD jail with nothing in it held fourteen tools that already handled alerting, history and forensics. Three packages later it weighed 976 megabytes more and could report that it was idle. Grafana's empty database is 265,768 kilobytes; the whole log directory of the same machine is 79. A staged incident took two commands and a minute to name its cause.

The NFS Under Your Cloud Bill

Sun had a furniture problem in 1984: engineers move between desks, and their files did not. The answer was to slide the network one layer below the file system, where no application had to look at it. It worked so well the protocol went invisible, and invisible things get rented back. Amazon EFS is NFS, at 0.30 dollars per gigabyte-month against 0.08 for block storage on the same platform.

Seven Lines of WireGuard

On FreeBSD the tunnel belongs to no package: pkg which finds nothing, because it came with the system. Seven lines of configuration, two keys of thirty-two bytes, and two independent teams who implemented the same specification to within seven per cent. Six years of kernel maintenance added ninety-two lines and never moved the file count.

Trust Does Not Travel

The most patient attack in software history took three years of good manners and never touched a firewall. It was aimed at a handover, and the handover worked as designed. Trust does not travel: at every station it is founded again by someone who answers for what they saw, or it quietly evaporates while the artefact keeps moving. Bridges get signed calculations and certified steel; software ships AS IS, in capital letters.
